And there's another threat, one that compounds the first threat:

* Component markets (needs not much of an introduction assuming you're reading in 2026)

### Why did I go with Windows? Am I stupid?
The main reason why I didn't go with a linux distro is just that... my desktop linux experience can be best described as "I *tried* to love it"

Now don't get me wrong, Linux gaming has gone a long way, and it's good to see, and I am someone who uses Linux, but only on servers.

I've tried desktop linux twice, first using Nobara (Fedora based distro), second using debian (with LXDE).

But the truth is, I just... don't really find myself loving it on the desktop, I often find myself having to do hacky workarounds for some games I play (i.e. FS2020, Genshin impact, and what if I want to (re)explore other games?), and as much as I love nerd stuff and tinkering, I just... I don't necessarily want to do all sorts of silly hacks just to get something to work.

Another is that I don't find file management particularly enjoyable on Linux, nor is opening stuff like archive files a somewhat important productivity task for me.

Ultimately, even though I enjoy nerding out and messing around, I just... I don't want to do it constantly for stuff I want to do at times, especially gaming.

### Why did you go Intel?
I am well aware that the consensus is AMD for... basically everything, they have more reliable products, cheaper products, their sockets last long, and they have an awesome X3D lineup and frankly.

But here, there was a legitimate defense to Intel, for several reasons:

- The 32GB DDR5 bundle for the 9700X and 270K Plus were priced similarly (sadly I did not check this at the time, *maybe* I'd have gone with AMD if I did) 
- However, the 270K Plus has 16 extra Ecores, which, as someone who uses her pc for non-gaming too, likes this.
- The 270K Plus and the 9700X (and even the 7800X3D) perform similarly in games, so I'm not losing out on much

So ultimately, even though AMD has absolutely blown Intel out of the water in terms of dominance, Intel is still not far behind, and IMO, that's a good thing, the competition between both ensures that no one CPU maker can dominate the market, or god forbid, starts stagnating.

#### On the motherboard:
So I did some research into the motherboard I got for my new PC (Gigabyte EAGLE Z890 WIFI7 PLUS), and I will say, for it being a so-called "budget to mid range board", it packs a lot of punch.
Here are some of the features I can see that make it feel like a high-end board:

- Hella metal on both sides, so it feels strong
- Plenty of M.2 slots, covered with, again, metal
- Rear Power + Reset + Clear CMOS + Q-flash buttons
- Built-in IO Shield
- Debug LEDs 
- Built-in Wifi7
- Here is a unique one: 4x USB 2.0 ports
    - It might seem strange, but there is a unique reason why I like this, and it has something to do with... low bandwidth peripherals! Mainly things like keyboard, mouse, headset, microphone, and webcams. The main reason why I like this is... it doesn't make sense to take up high-bandwidth USB 3.0 or 3.1 ports for things like keyboards and mice, yet my previous motherboard only had 2 USB 2.0 ports. What's nice here is that low-bandwidth stuff can be plugged into the USB 2.0 ports and I don't have to waste bandwidth

That said, there are some things I feel as if could be improved:

- More SATA ports, I feel like that 4, even though for most users would likely be enough, isn't enough for everything and everyone, what if I want to repurpose this into a server one day?
- More PCIE slots, 1x16 slot and 2x 4x-but-16x physical slots IMO isn't enough for things that might be installed, even if M.2 slots are plentiful and GPUs are getting thicker
- A more intuitive BIOS (like ASUS's) and not having Secure Boot on by default
- The thing that grinds my gears the most, however, is that the reset button is recessed, but either the Q-flash button... isn't?
- A Postcode readout for better debugging

#### The GPU story:
This one is a funny story, because it was one of those "last minute oh shit" decisions (I literally had got most of the parts), as one would say. I was initially thinking of just... using my RTX 2070 Super as my GPU for now, and honestly, it was a good card, sure it 's not blazing fast anymore, but it's not bad. But, I kind of just decided to go GPU shopping... anyway. At the micro center (best computer store ever), I saw the refurbished listings for some refurb cards, I considered them, but then I realized that they would likely not come back for a while, if ever. I then decided to walk up to the DIY section to ask if they had any refurb GPUs, iirc they said they had a 7800 XT for 475,whichIwashesitantforandquicklydecidedagainst.SoafterIgothomethatnight,IdecidedtotryandfindaGPUforadecentprice,noluck.ButthenIdecidedtoexpandmysearch,andIstumbledupona625USDeBaylistingforanRX7900XTReferenceGPU,withonlyoneissue:poortemperatures,especiallyduringextendedgaming.Iwasabithesitant,decidedtotrylookingelsewhereforaGPUinthe5-600 range (my traditional range), and I found... not much, some looked promising but I wasn't sure if I should go with them. So I decided to come back to the 7900XT listing from eBay, when I got an offer for 575$. Initially, I decided to try and get it down even further, but the seller refused, so I decided to accept it. It works well, temperatures are fine from my testing, fans work, etc etc, and I will repaste it when I get the chance (ft. PTM7950).

However, the RX 7900 XT is actually, very much a good card for the price I paid. It has a lot of horsepower AND 20GB VRAM, very good compared to most NVIDIA (or even consumer AMD cards with RDNA 4), and despite it being 4 years old, it's only last generation (and high end at that), and it supports tech like FSR4.1, so IMO, it was a good call that I didn't sacrifice too much on. Perf-wise, it trades blows with the RTX 5070 and RX 9070, and surpasses the 9070 GRE and 5060 TI. 

That isn't to say that the card is perfect, it's ovbiously not a 5080/90, and there is one drawback that is inherit to this generation (RDNA 3), that being, worse ray tracing compared to NVIDIA GPUs

That said, I will say that, for the price I paid for, I got a great deal on a card that not only has comparable performance to cards that are *not* going to be sold for 575$, but actually has things *beyond* those cards, like VRAM.
#### Installing Windows:
A brief story here, but for some reason, I *always* fail to install Windows on the first try when building PCs. 

For my first PC:

- I'd get the error of "Windows detected that the EFI system partition was formatted as NTFS. Format the EFI system partition as FAT32, and restart the installation." at first when installing Windows
- So, I (probably wrongfully), formated the USB drive as FAT32 and used a tool to turn an ISO to that USB, but then... I got the "Windows cannot open the required file D:\Sources\install.wim. Make sure that all files required for installation are available and restart the installation. Error code: 0x8007000D" error. It turns out, FAT32 has a filesize limit, and then-modern WIM images exceed it.
- Eventually, after numerous trials and error (thanks dad), I got the installer working

For this PC, I had a similar challenge, it turns out, despite literally running a Windows ISO Site, I had a similar mistake. 

I kept trying to boot into IOT Enterprise LTSC (using Ventoy), and I kept getting errors. I tried wimboot, nothing, I tried legacy and UEFI, nothing, I tried different partition tables, filesystems, etc, and nothing. 

Then, it hit me, I had downloaded an ARM64 ISO instead of an AMD64 one. So I had to download an AMD64 one and copy *that*, 🫠 and install windows. Then, I had to try to install Windows twice, the first time failing, so I had to use the older setup UI.

I eventually got Windows installed, and from then on, very little issues. I am now enjoying my PC, using it for gaming, and more!
