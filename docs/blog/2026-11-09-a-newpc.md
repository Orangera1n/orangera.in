### Why did I go with Windows? Am I stupid?
The main reason why I didn't go with a linux distro is just that... my desktop linux experience can be best described as "I *tried* to love it"

Now don't get me wrong, Linux gaming has gone a long way, and it's good to see, and I am someone who uses Linux, but only on servers.

I've tried desktop linux twice, first using Nobara (Fedora based distro), second using debian (with LXDE).

But truth is, I just... don't really find myself loving it on the desktop, I often find myself having to do hacky workarounds for some games I play (i.e. FS2020, Genshin impact, and what if i want to (re)explore other games?), and as much as I love nerd stuff and tinkering, I just... don't nessesarily want to do all sorts of silly hacks just to get something to work.

Another is that I don't find file management particulary enjoyable on Linux, nor is opening stuff like archive files, a somewhat important productivity task for me.

Ultimately, even though I enjoy nerding out and messing around, I just... don't want to do it constantly for stuff I want to do at times, espically gaming.

### Why did you go Intel?
I am well aware that the consensus is AMD for... basically everything, they have more reliable products, cheapaer products, their sockets last long, and they have an awesome X3D lineup and frankly.

But here, there was a legitimate defense to Intel, for several reasons:

- The 32GB DDR5 bundle for the 9700X and 270K Plus were priced simialry (sadly I did not check this at the time, *maybe* I'd have gone with AMD if I did) 
- However, the 270K Plus has 16 extra Ecores, which, as someone who uses her pc for non-gaming too, likes this.
- The 270K Plus and the 9700X (and even the 7800X3D) perform similary in games, so I'm not loosing out on much

So ultimately, even though AMD has absolutely blown Intel out of the water in terms of dommance, Intel is still not far behind, and IMO, thats a good thing, the competition between both ensures that no one CPU maker can dominate the market, or god forbid, starts stagnating.

#### The motherboard:
So I did some research into the motherboard I got for my new PC (Gigabyte EAGLE Z890 WIFI7 PLUS), and I will say, for it being a so called "budget to mid range board", it packs alot of punch.
Here are some of the features I can see that make it feel like a sufficentalyl good board:

- Hella metal on both sides, so it feels strong
- Plenty of M.2 slots, covered with, again, metal
- Rear Power + Reset + Clear CMOS + Q-flash buttons
- Built-in IO Sheild
- Debug LEDs 
- Built-in Wifi7
- Here is a unique one: 4x USB 2.0 ports, and theres a unique reason why I like this: non-drive peripherals, things like keyboard, mouse, headset, microphone, and webcams. The main reason why i like this is... it doesn't make sense to take up high-bandwidth USB 3.0 or 3.1 ports for things like keyboards and mice, so having 4x USB 2 ports for low-bandwidth devices makes sense.

That said, there are some things I feel as if could be improved:

- More SATA ports, I feel like that 4, even thoguh for most users is enough, isn't enough for everything
- More PCIE slots, 1x16 slot and 2x 4x-but-16x phsyically slots IMO isn't enough for things that might be installed, even if M.2 slots are plentiful and GPUs are getting thicker
- A more intutive BIOS (like ASUS's) and not having Secure Boot on by default
- The thing that grinds my gears the most, however, is that the reset button is recessed, but either the Q-flash button... isn't?
- A Postcode readout for better debugging
